## What the comparison establishes

The offline runner fits one multiplicative response scale on 35 sols (5–39). It then compares predictions with 161 later operational energy estimates (40–200). Four early sols have no bracketing opacity and remain unmodelled. Atmospheric input is a retrospective interpolation of camera retrievals, limited to 15-sol brackets. Source sigma is retained in the connector but not propagated into a confidence interval.

The dust-aware model returns 289.5 Wh/sol RMSE and +255.0 Wh/sol bias; geometry-only returns 1327.6 Wh/sol RMSE. This supports including deposited dust but **does not establish mission-design accuracy**. The dust rate comes from a paper on the same mission, and the energy series consists of fitted operational estimates. Holding out energy values alone does not make this independent validation of the published model. The fitted scale absorbs unmeasured temperature, spectrum and electrical operating conditions; it is not an inferred cell efficiency or nameplate capacity.

## Priority next steps

1. Couple a spectral/multiple-scattering solver or licensed lookup tables to the component interface, then benchmark against Crisp/Kerr results.
2. Add spatial Mars dust climatology with explicit wavelength/pressure conversions, and thermal boundary inputs from appropriate mission/climate products.
3. Fit/validate cell-temperature and electrical operating-point effects using independent measurements. Current DBR strings cannot simply be multiplied into whole-array energy.
4. Evaluate cleaning events and deposition variation on a different mission/year; report uncertainty and held-out bias before optimizing hardware or storage.
